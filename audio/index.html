    Audio output (I2S, test signals, WAV, FLAC, MP3, ALAC)¶

    Phase 1 sound comes out of the Pimoroni Pico Audio Pack ("the Audio Pack DAC") over I2S, driven by the PIO and DMA from the bare-metal audio core (ADR-0011, Runtime). The audio policy is ADR-0003: decode once to a 32-bit mixer, no resampling; the sink is re-clocked per file instead.

    Pins¶

    Signal GPIO Notes
    I2S DATA GP9 PIO0 out pin
    I2S BCK GP10 PIO0 side-set bit 0
    I2S LRCK GP11 PIO0 side-set bit 1 (BCK and LRCK must be consecutive)
    Mute / amp enable GP22 plain GPIO, driven by core 1 (and by core 0 at boot and in the fatal-error path)

    The Audio Pack is a TI PCM5100A DAC with a PAM8908 headphone amp behind the "phones" jack (low/high gain switch); "line out" is the DAC output directly. Try both jacks.

    No MCLK: the PCM5100A runs its internal PLL from BCK, which must be 32, 48 or 64 × fs with a 50 % LRCK. Our 32-bit slots give 64 fs. SCK is tied low on the Pack; the format pin is hardwired to standard I2S (one-BCK delay), which is what neupod_i2s.pio produces.

    Resource allocation¶

    Resource Used by Notes
    PIO0, 1 state machine, 8 instructions I2S transmitter (platforms/rp2350/hal/neupod_i2s.pio) claimed with pio_claim_unused_sm, program added and removed at open/close. pio1 = SD (carlk3), pio2 = CYW43 + click wheel
    2 DMA channels (claimed dynamically) ping-pong, chained A to B to A, 512 words each (256 stereo frames) high bus priority; read pointer wraps inside its 2 KB buffer (ring), so a late restart replays the old block instead of reading past the buffer
    DMA_IRQ_1, enabled on core 1 block-complete interrupt, refills the finished buffer DMA_IRQ_0 stays with the SD library on core 0
    2 x 2 KB DMA buffers, 32 KB PCM ring, 32 KB data ring RAM PCM ring size is NEUPOD_PCM_RING_FRAMES in app/os/memory_budget.h; data ring in audio_shared.c
    Hot path in RAM dma_isr, on_block, audio_apply_gain, the ring functions, hal_audio_write, hal_audio_mute NEUPOD_RAM_FUNC / __not_in_flash_func

    The sink is chosen at build time: scripts/build.sh rp2350 -- -DNEUPOD_AUDIO_SINK=i2s (default) or null (the old discard-on-a-clock sink, touches no pins). The host build uses SDL for audible output when SDL2 and an audio device exist (NEUPOD_HOST_AUDIO=0 forces silence), otherwise it is the null sink.

    On the Innioasis Y2 the wired sink is the headphone jack's CS43131 DAC (Y2, Audio): the audio core is unchanged (same 32-bit blocks, same software volume and ramps); the HAL converts to 16 bit (TPDF dither whenever the volume or a deeper source leaves bits below the 16th), queues the blocks in a ring, and a writer thread paces the whole player on the DAC's clock. 44.1 kHz only, with the null sink as the fallback when no headphones are plugged in. hal_audio_wired_attached() tells the audio route whether anything is on the jack (always true on the other platforms): headphones pulled out while playing pause the player with a "Headphones unplugged" banner. The Y2's internal speaker and Line out use the same sink: hal_audio_set_speaker() / hal_audio_set_line_out() carry the route's decision (Settings > Speaker, Settings > Line out, Audio output Auto / Wired / Bluetooth / Speaker) to the HAL, hal_audio_headphones_in() and hal_audio_speaker_available() / hal_audio_line_out_available() report the hardware; the other platforms answer no. The amplifier-off-first rules, the volume caps (speaker 9, jack 11, Line out 15) and the allow-lists are in Y2. Line out locks the software volume at 0 dB (player_volume_set_locked()).

    Interrupt flow¶

    1. Start: both buffers hold silence; DMA channel A is started, the PIO is enabled.
    2. When a channel finishes it chains to the other one and raises DMA_IRQ_1.
    3. The handler acknowledges, then for the finished buffer calls the block callback (on_block in app/audio/audio_core.c), which reads 256 frames from the PCM ring, applies volume, writes them into the buffer through hal_audio_write. The handler re-arms that channel's count.
    4. Ring empty: that block is silence and underruns counts it. If the interrupt runs so late that both buffers finished, dma_underruns (sink_missed) counts it and the old block is replayed once. If the late interrupt finds the other buffer's channel already restarted, the finished sibling is re-armed with its old block (never left at a zero count, which would end the ping-pong).
    5. 32 starved blocks in a row (186 ms) while playing: playback stops, the output is muted and the event STORM is logged.

    Sink restart (re-clock on a sample-rate change)¶

    Close and hal_audio_force_mute() stop the sink in a fixed order, registers only and with bounded waits: mute pin; (close only) DMA interrupt off; clear the EN bit of both channels through the non-triggering AL1_CTRL alias (so the chain cannot re-trigger the sibling, RP2350-E5); abort both and wait for CHAN_ABORT and BUSY to clear; disable their INTE1 bits and acknowledge INTS1; disable the state machine, clear its FIFOs, restart it and its clock divider, clear its FDEBUG flags. It is safe to call twice or with nothing open. Open starts from the same clean state (the claimed channels are quiesced, pio_sm_init), configures both channels, clears INTS1 and the pending NVIC bit, enables the interrupt, then starts channel A and enables the state machine. Before this fix a rate switch could leave channel A armed and busy while the PIO requested data and nothing moved (audio core stalled). The dma_underruns=2 seen once per reopen is gone too; the likely explanation is the two stale chained channels completing after the unwaited abort (consistent with the counter now reading 0, not proven). An abort that has not completed within the spin bound (about 2 ms) is counted (g_abort_timeouts, readable over SWD) and makes open return HAL_EBUSY rather than configure a running channel. In the late-interrupt path, if both channels are found idle after re-arming the sibling, the sibling is restarted (the ping-pong had stopped).

    Bench, 3 Nov 2026 (Pico 2 W): 12 ALAC 44.1 ↔ 96 kHz switches while playing, a switch from a paused 96 kHz stream and an ALAC 96 kHz → WAV 44.1 kHz switch: no stall, dma_underruns=0 after every reopen (was 2 every time).

    Clocking and rate accuracy¶

    The PIO runs at 128 x Fs: two PIO cycles per BCK, 64 BCK per frame (32-bit slots). The divider comes from clk_sys = 125 MHz in 1/256 steps (16.8 fixed point), rounded to nearest.

    Fs divider achieved Fs error BCK PIO cycle
    44.1 kHz 22 + 37/256 44099.488 Hz -11.6 ppm 2.8224 MHz 177 ns
    48 kHz 20 + 88/256 48003.072 Hz +64 ppm 3.0722 MHz 163 ns
    88.2 kHz 11 + 18/256 88214.538 Hz +165 ppm 5.6457 MHz 89 ns
    96 kHz 10 + 44/256 96006.144 Hz +64 ppm 6.1444 MHz 81 ns

    The monitor line audio sink: prints the actual rate and divider read back from the hardware setup. Consequences:

    • The DAC slaves to BCK, so a rate error is not a sync problem; it only changes pitch and duration, by at most 165 ppm (0.29 cent; a 4-minute track is 40 ms shorter or longer). Not audible.
    • The fractional divider dithers each PIO cycle between 22 and 23 system clocks (and so on), so a BCK edge moves by at most one system clock, 8 ns, around its ideal place: 2 % of a 354 ns BCK period at 44.1 kHz, 5 % at 96 kHz. A DAC that regenerates its clock with a PLL (as no-MCLK DACs do) filters this. Not measured on the board; an oscilloscope on GP10 would show it.
    • 88.2 and 96 kHz are generated by the same code and accepted by the WAV player, but were not run on hardware.

    Mute line (GP22)¶

    • GP22 drives the PCM5100A's XSMT pin (TSSOP-20 pin 17), active LOW: low = soft-muted, high = playing (design response, 3 Nov 2026, from the Pack schematic; confirmed on the bench the same day). The build default NEUPOD_AUDIO_UNMUTE_LEVEL=1 is correct.
    • The DAC also auto-mutes on a clock error: BCK and LRCK must be running continuously, and its PLL locked, before XSMT goes high.
    • Current behaviour (as built): muted from boot (hal_system_init), at sink open and close, on stop, on an underrun storm and in the fatal-error path (hal_panic → hal_audio_force_mute(), which also aborts the DMA and stops the PIO). The audio core opens XSMT one block before a fade-in and closes it after two silent blocks (app/audio/audio_core.c), and hal_audio_close() stops the I2S clocks between streams. So XSMT toggles with every start/stop. The stream is opened at boot and its clocks keep running, so in practice XSMT rises long after the PLL has locked.
    • Bench, 3 Nov 2026: works. 1 kHz test tone heard on the "line out" jack (DAC direct, no PAM8908) at -30 dB. The earlier silence was wiring: the GP9 (DATA) and GP22 (XSMT) jumpers were on the wrong Pico pins. Register read-back over SWD (SIO_IN / IO_BANK0 STATUS) showed both pins driven correctly while the Pack end read 0 V, which is how it was found.
    • Design recommendation (optional, not built): raise XSMT once after the clocks have settled and leave it high, doing start/stop/pause with the gain ramp only; XSMT low only for force-mute (panic, storm) and sink close. Not needed for function on the testbed; it reduces DAC soft-mute ramps at track boundaries.

    Volume safety¶

    • Volume is attenuation in whole dB, applied in the 32-bit domain. Start-up level is -30 dB and is sent to the audio core before anything can play; the audio core also boots at -30 dB by itself. The volume is saved on the card (settings store, Bluetooth) together with the output path, and restored at boot by the Volume restore policy: the saved level on the same known path, otherwise capped at -30 dB. Nothing reports the output path yet, so today every restore is capped at -30 dB, as before.
    • The UI steps 2 dB per wheel click. The engine never applies gain above 0 dB, and a build-time hard cap (NEUPOD_AUDIO_VOLUME_CAP_DB, default 0, meaning full scale allowed) sets the loudest level; raise it for a headphone-safe build. At 80 dB or more the output is silent.
    • The test tone is -20 dBFS; with the default volume it reaches the DAC at -50 dBFS peak. The Test tone screen prints that figure. Leaving the Test tone screen stops the sound.
    • Volume changes, pause, resume, start and stop are ramped linearly across one 5.8 ms block (no clicks).

    Bit-perfect note¶

    PCM files are converted to 32 bits exactly (16-bit << 16, 24-bit << 8, 32-bit as is; mono duplicated to both channels), with no dither and no resampling. At 0 dB volume the samples then go to the DAC unchanged (verified by the unit tests; the gain stage is skipped). Only 0 dB is bit-perfect: any other setting multiplies the samples. The first and last block of a start/stop/pause are ramped even at 0 dB. The DAC itself and its analogue stage are not "bit-perfect" in any measurable sense; the claim is about the digital path. The Audio Pack DAC takes 32-bit slots; how many of the bits it uses is the DAC's business.

    A different sample rate re-clocks the sink (close and reopen, a few ms) with no resampling. The tones run at the sink's current rate (44.1 kHz after boot, or the last file's rate).     Format Decoder Licence Runs on Output

    MP3 (MPEG-1/2 Layer III, 44.1/48 kHz) dr_mp3 (contains minimp3) public domain / MIT-0 (minimp3 CC0) core 1 float, rounded to nearest and clipped to 32 bits

    dr_wav is vendored with the others as requested but is not compiled: the WAV path has no codec in it, only a header parser that we already have and test, so dr_wav would add code without adding capability. More than 2 channels are rejected with a message, as are unsupported rates ("Unsupported sample rate"); mono is played on both sides.

    Where things run¶

    • Core 0 (storage task) reads the file, parses the header and tags, and streams bytes through the 32 KB data ring:
      • FLAC: a synthesised 42-byte header (fLaC + one STREAMINFO block) followed by the audio frames, so the decoder never has to stream through the file's pictures and comments. Tags (Vorbis comments) are read here.
      • MP3: the compressed bytes from the first frame header on (ID3v2 skipped and parsed here; duration from the Xing/Info frame count, otherwise estimated from the file size, which can be far off for VBR files that lack one; shown with a ~).
      • M4A: the container is demuxed here (it needs random access to moov): app/audio/mp4.c reads stsd (the ALAC magic cookie), stsz, stsc, stco/co64, mdhd and the ilst tags, and sends each packet with a 4-byte big-endian length in front. The demuxer is codec independent so AAC can use it later.
      • Pictures: the tag readers do not load them. They record where each embedded picture is (audio_tags_t.art: file offset, length, kind JPEG / PNG / other, picture type) from the MP3 ID3v2 APIC / PIC frame, the FLAC PICTURE block and the MP4 covr item, reading only the few header bytes that say where the image starts and its first four bytes to tell JPEG from PNG: a 2 MB PNG costs the same few reads as a 2 KB one. A JPEG is preferred to a PNG, a front cover (type 3) to other types. Unsynchronised, compressed or encrypted ID3 frames cannot be streamed and are ignored. The art itself is decoded afterwards by the storage task (see UI).
    • Core 1 decodes. The main loop calls the decoder for up to two 256-frame blocks per pass while the PCM ring has room; the block interrupt is unchanged and still preempts it. Everything the decoders allocate comes from one static arena of 116 KB (codec.h), reset at every new stream: malloc is never called (the vendored allocation macros point at the arena), asserts are compiled out, and there is no stdio. tools/check_core1_closure.py (Python is required by the RP2350 build; ctest also runs the checker against a known-good and a known-bad fixture, so it is proven able to fail) runs after every RP2350 build and fails it if anything reachable from the audio core's entry points could reach the RTOS, stdio, the heap, blocking SDK calls or the panic path (it walks the call graph of the linked image, following branches and every function address found in literal pools; a function whose address is only ever computed would be missed, see the limits in the script's header); the audio_core_rules token scan still covers our own sources. Core 1's stack is now a 16 KB array of our own (minimp3 alone keeps about 10 KB of scratch on the stack).
    • Waiting for data: the decoder's read callback blocks until the bytes are there (core 0 refills at about 2 MB/s). While it waits it keeps handling commands: volume, pause and the end-of-stream marker immediately; anything that tears the stream down (stop, a new track, a tone) is parked, the read is abandoned and the command runs next, so nothing can deadlock. 32 starved blocks in a row still stops and mutes the output.
    • The first block of audio is not played until about 23 ms are buffered (and a stream is complete if it is shorter), so a slow start is silence rather than an underrun.
    • Seeking inside the decoder (the Now Playing seek is different, see "Seeking to a frame" below): forward seeks inside the decoder (including dr_flac's own) discard bytes; an absolute seek backwards sends core 0 a request (byte offset), core 0 repositions the file and acknowledges, core 1 then empties the data ring and acknowledges back, and only then does core 0 write again (stream_io.h; unit-tested with a real second thread).

    Gapless playback and the play queue¶

    Decision record: ADR-0016. The queue is the playable files of the folder a track was chosen from, in Files order; the player (core 0) owns it. Gapless is on by default (Settings, "Gapless playback").

    Handshake. Every stream core 0 starts or announces gets a sequence number; its description (audio_stream_desc_t) and its length in bytes live in g_audio_slot[seq & 1]. The data ring carries streams back to back.

    1. Hard start (audio_stream_start, command STREAM_START, a = sequence number): core 1 flushes both rings, re-clocks the sink if the rate differs, ramps, as before. It cancels any announcement.
    2. Once the current decoder is open (core 1 publishes g_audio_open_seq), the storage task asks the player which track follows, opens and parses it (the rate and channel count must match) and announces it: audio_stream_next() fills the other slot, sets g_audio_next_valid and sends STREAM_NEXT. Core 1 only remembers the number (it handles this command while waiting for data too).
    3. When the current track is completely in the ring, audio_stream_end() publishes its byte count in its slot and the announced track's bytes follow immediately. Core 1's byte source (sio_t.end) reads the ring first and the length second, so whenever it sees the next stream's bytes it also sees the boundary; reads never cross it, and the decoder sees the end of its stream there. Codecs that stop early because of trimming (see below) skip the rest of the stream up to the boundary.
    4. The decoder returns its last frame: core 1 looks at the command ring once more (an announcement sent just before the end is not missed), then, if the announcement is still valid (g_audio_next_valid re-read after copying the description), closes the decoder, opens the next one on the same sink, PCM ring and volume (the decoders' byte source g_sio is rebuilt for every stream, WAV included, so a WAV that hands over to a codec track never opens it on an unset source), and pushes the number of PCM frames written so far into a small boundary ring. The block interrupt counts the frames it serves; when they reach the boundary it reports END_OF_TRACK with value 1 and elapsed restarts. So the event fires in the block that carries the old track's last sample (within 256 frames), not when decoding ends. No mute change, no ramp.
    5. Nothing announced (last track, gapless off, other rate or channel count, unreadable file, a track too short to chain): the track ends as before (ring drains, ramp, END_OF_TRACK value 0) and the player starts the next one with a hard start.

    Seeks: a decoder only seeks while it opens, when nothing can be announced yet. A seek request nevertheless withdraws an announcement and the end marker (core 0), and g_audio_next_valid makes a stale STREAM_NEXT harmless. Stop, skip or a new start clear g_audio_next_valid before anything reuses a slot; core 1 drops its pending note on stop. Only one handoff may be pending at a time. A different format is refused by core 1 too (error event, the track ends normally).

    Encoder delay and padding. FLAC and WAV lengths are exact. MP3: with a LAME tag dr_mp3 drops delay + 529 frames at the start and padding - 529 at the end; the parser reports exactly the length it will deliver, and the codec layer does not trim again. Without a LAME tag nothing is trimmed (and a missing frame count gives an estimate, shown with ~). ALAC/M4A: the edit list (elst, one non-empty entry: media_time = priming, segment_duration = length) is preferred, else iTunSMPB (hex: priming, padding, original length). A trim that does not fit the media is ignored. The reported total_frames is the trimmed length. The test vectors: tools/gen_trim_vectors.py.

    Host tests. gapless runs the real core-1 loop and the real core-0 stream controller against a fake sink with a real-time block clock and compares the sink's samples frame by frame with each track decoded on its own (WAV, FLAC, MP3, ALAC with an edit list; skip, stop, seek and rate change cases). play_queue covers the queue rules (including shuffle permutations, repeat, wrap re-shuffle) and player the mid-play toggles.

    Shuffle and repeat (Settings > Shuffle / Repeat, saved; Now Playing shows small shuffle/loop icons, loop with a 1 for repeat-one): see the amendment in ADR-0016. UART reasons added: repeat, wrap (and gapless repeat, gapless wrap).

    Bench, 3 Nov 2026 (Pico 2 W): 30-signals/gapless/ (3-part FLAC then 3-part LAME MP3, one queue): five (gapless) handoffs incl. FLAC → MP3, one END_OF_TRACK(1) per boundary, sink never re-clocked, mute line open throughout, 0 underruns. Next / Previous (restart when more than 3 s in, previous track within 3 s) / Next on the last track (queue end) correct; a skip while the successor was being announced leaves later joins gapless. Gapless Off: (auto) joins with the ramp, setting persists across a reboot. 44.1 → 48 kHz in 10-matrix/: (auto) with a re-clock, dma_underruns=0, 0 underruns.

    Resume (last track and position)¶

    The player (core 0) remembers the folder track that was playing and where (app/runtime/resume.c, two settings items NPr0/NPr1: track path, folder entry index, 64-bit file size, position in PCM frames, playing or paused; the entry, not the shuffle slot, is saved). It is stored when the user pauses or resumes, when a track starts (also a gapless or repeat one) and on Stop (the place is kept). Under repeat-all or repeat-one the album never ends, so the record is never cleared; without repeat the end of the album clears it. These calls only change the RAM image (serialised by a small mutex, as the UI and the storage task both call them); the SD write is the settings store's own save on the storage task (write lock opened only inside that save, nothing on core 1).

    There is no periodic update by default. The rule "no SD writes while audio plays" stands, and a mid-track update could only be written during playback: the write runs on the storage task that also refills the audio data ring, so a card that stalls for 100-250 ms for one write would underrun a high-rate stream (at 24-bit/96 kHz the rings hold only about 85-130 ms). So a power cut mid-track resumes at the last pause, resume, Stop or track start, not at the exact second. An opt-in build exception exists (RESUME_WRITE_WHILE_PLAYING 1, resume.h; default 0, unbenched): it adds a 30 s update (RESUME_PERIOD_MS) and writes it during playback only when the data ring plus the PCM ring hold at least 300 ms of audio (judged pessimistically as 32-bit stereo, so it rarely opens), times each such write, and turns itself off for the boot if one takes over 50 ms. There is no orderly-shutdown hook in the firmware yet (no power-off path); pausing before switching off stores the exact place.

    At boot, right after the settings load, the storage task lists the saved folder (only if the UI has not listed anything yet: the listing buffer is shared), resume_plan() decides, and the player opens the track (in a queue built with the saved shuffle and repeat modes: under shuffle the order is new, the track is the saved one) paused at the saved frame (nothing is played until the user presses play): the file is there with the same size: that frame; same name but another size: the same track from the beginning; the file is gone: the playable entry at the saved queue position, from its beginning; the folder cannot be read, or holds nothing playable, or the track cannot be opened: the record is discarded.

    Resume playing on power-up (Settings, NPra, u8, default Off; player_resume_autoplay()): Off keeps the behaviour above (always paused). On: the same restore, but when the saved record says it was playing (resume_state_t.playing) the track starts playing instead of being paused (player_resume_folder(..., play)); a record that says paused or stopped still restores paused. The setting is read after the settings load and the volume restore (same task, in that order), so auto-play uses the restored volume, including the -30 dB cap for an unknown or different output path. A track that cannot be opened is dropped quietly in both modes.

    Seeking to a frame (track_feed_seek(), core 0 only; the one core-1 change is that the FLAC decoder honours skip_frames):

    Codec Start position
    WAV exact frame (the byte)
    ALAC (M4A) exact frame: the packet is found with the sample tables (mp4_iter_seek: the stsc runs and one chunk, not every packet before it) and the decoder drops the frames before the target; trimming (edit list, iTunSMPB) stays exact
    FLAC exact frame if the file has a SEEKTABLE (nearest earlier seek point, the decoder drops the rest); without one the track starts at the beginning (position 0)
    MP3 nearest frame: from the Xing table of contents or the Fraunhofer VBRI table (VBR) or the byte proportion (CBR, within a frame; VBR roughly 1%; a file with no frame count at all uses an estimate from its first frame's bit rate), then a resync to a frame header confirmed by the one after it. The position that is then shown and saved is computed back from where the frame header really landed, so errors do not add up over repeated resumes. The first granule after the jump may be silent (bit reservoir) and the LAME end padding is no longer trimmed

    tools/gen_seek_vector.py makes the FLAC vector with a seek table for the tests (tests/test_gapless.c, through the real core-1 loop).

    Seek from Now Playing (player_seek(), UI-7): the same file is started again at the target frame, a hard start exactly like the boot resume, so core 1 sees an ordinary new stream and nothing new crosses between the cores. Consequences: a short mute (the ramp-down, the ring flush and the decoder opening; the audio core's underrun and storm counters are not involved); an announced gapless successor is withdrawn by the stop and the player's counters are re-based, and the stream controller announces it again for the restarted stream once its ring is full, so the handoff at the end of the track stays sample-exact (tests/test_gapless.c, seek during prefetch); paused stays paused (the restarted stream is paused before anything is fed). Only tracks whose track_info_t.seekable is set are offered it: WAV, ALAC, MP3 (UI-7: the scrub bar and the time show where an MP3 really landed, so a VBR file moves in jumps of up to about 1 % of the track), and FLAC with a SEEKTABLE and a known length (it is exact; without a table the only way would be decoding from the start; such a FLAC answers Seek not available). A target at or past the last frame is the end of the track: the queue advances as it does when a track plays out (repeat-one restarts it, the last track stops). Scrubbing applies one restart per rest of the wheel (0.35 s), not one per step. The player counts the start requests it has posted (the storage task answers each exactly once, in order) and applies only the answer to the last one, so a seek followed by another seek, Next or a seek to the end never shows the replaced start's track data; the answer to a replaced start that fails is ignored too. When a seek's restart is up, the new position is saved for resume once (RESUME_EVENT), not per scrub step.

    Metadata¶

    FLAC Vorbis comments (TITLE, ARTIST, ALBUM, TRACKNUMBER), ID3v2.2/2.3/2.4 text frames (TIT2, TPE1, TALB, TRCK; ISO-8859-1, UTF-16 and UTF-8), and MP4 ilst (©nam, ©ART, ©alb, trkn) are parsed on core 0 and shown on Now Playing with the format badge (FLAC 24/96, MP3 320k or MP3 VBR ~192k, ALAC 16/44.1) and the elapsed and remaining time. The screen font has no accented letters, so those show as an empty box. Embedded art is not read yet.

    FLAC verify mode (check without listening)¶

    In Extras > Files, press Right on a .flac (Select plays it). The decoder runs flat out with no sound, hashes every decoded sample the way FLAC defines its checksum (each sample as ceil(bits/8) little-endian bytes, channels interleaved) and compares it with the MD5 in the file's STREAMINFO at the end of the file. Now Playing shows MD5 OK, MD5 MISMATCH or No MD5 stored in this file, and the UART prints verify: '<name>' MD5 OK. A mismatch means the data from the card or the decoder is wrong, so it tests the whole path from the card to the decoder. (An MD5 of all zeros means the encoder did not compute one.)

    Robustness¶

    Files are untrusted input. The header parsers bound every length and walk (box sizes are compared without wrapping, a box budget of 20,000, vendor and comment lengths checked, text conversion never writes past its buffer); ALAC rejects a partial-frame sample count above its frame length (the original decoder trusted it); an MP4 packet outside the file or larger than the decoder's frame buffer ends the stream; a decode that ends short of the announced length is an error ("File is truncated or damaged"), so a damaged FLAC can never report MD5 OK or Finished; and a codec error makes core 0 stop reading the card. Once a command has interrupted the decoder's wait, every further read fails until the engine has handled it. Core 1's stack has a hardware limit (MSPLIM) 256 bytes above its bottom, so an overflow faults instead of corrupting RAM. The tests truncate every vector at each early byte and at 40 further points and flip random bits (fixed seed) in all of FLAC, MP3 and M4A; -DNEUPOD_HOST_ASAN=ON adds test_codecs_asan, the same tests under AddressSanitizer and UBSan (signed-overflow and shift reports are switched off: the vendored decoders wrap on corrupt data, which is harmless on ARM), and test_gapless_asan, the gapless test (the real core-1 loop, every codec pair) under the same sanitizers.

    Test vectors¶

    tests/assets/audio/ holds small FLAC, MP3 and M4A files with the MD5 of their source samples, made by tools/gen_audio_vectors.py (libsndfile via uv run --no-project --with soundfile --with numpy) and tools/gen_alac_vectors.py (Apple's reference ALAC encoder built from tests/tools/alac_encoder/ with g++, plus a hand-written MP4 muxer). ctest decodes every vector and compares it bit for bit (FLAC 16-bit 44.1 kHz, 24-bit 96 kHz and 48 kHz, mono; ALAC 16-bit, 24-bit with co64, mono), checks the MP3's length and level, the demuxer's sample-table maths, the tag parsers, MD5 against the RFC 1321 vectors, the arena (including the worst-case sizes) and the core 0 / core 1 stream handshake.

    Album art (tests/test_art.c, tests/test_art_snapshot.c, ctest art, art_tour, art_snapshot; pictures in tests/assets/art/, regenerated by tests/tools/gen_art_assets.py and tests/tools/make_art_flacs.py): decoded size, scale and quadrant colours of 4:2:0 600x600 / 500x333 / 1000x700, 4:4:4, 4:2:2 at an odd size, grey, restart markers and a tiny picture; progressive refused; truncated, junk and bit-flipped input; reads of 512 bytes at most and never past the picture; abort through the yield hook; cover-file ranking and the source order; APIC / PIC / PICTURE / covr positions including a 2 MB PNG and a 1.5 MB APIC that are not read; Now Playing snapshots with a cover file, an embedded JPEG, and art that cannot be shown.

    How to test (bench)¶

    1. Plug the Audio Pack, headphones off your head (or very low volume). Flash neupod.elf (scripts/build.sh rp2350, then scripts/flash.sh). The splash shows the audio icon solid if the PIO and DMA stream started (the DAC cannot be detected: I2S is one-way).
    2. UART keys: w/s scroll, e select, m back, a/d left/right (volume on the Test tone screen), p play/pause.
    3. Home: s x3 to Extras, e; s to Test tone, e. Select (e) on 1 kHz tone. You should hear a quiet tone; d raises the volume 1 step = 2 dB. Try the "phones" jack (gain switch low first) and the "line out" jack. If nothing is audible see Mute line — the polarity is confirmed, so do not flip NEUPOD_AUDIO_UNMUTE_LEVEL.
    4. e again stops it. Try L/R ident (left ear first), then Scale.
    5. Files: Extras > Files, a folder with a .wav, .flac, .mp3 or .m4a, Select. Now Playing opens with the tags and format badge; the wheel (w/s) is volume; p pauses; m goes back while it keeps playing. d (Right) on a .flac runs the MD5 verify instead of playing. An .m4a with AAC says "AAC not supported yet".
    6. Album art (see UI): play tracks from folders with a cover.jpg, from 20-tags (APIC files) and from 10-matrix/19-flac-16-44-bigpng.flac (PNG: placeholder, no stall) and 20-flac-16-44-smalljpg.flac; the UART prints art: ... with the source, size, decoder scale and time. Play ALAC 24/96 while art loads: underruns must stay 0.
    7. The UART prints every 10 s the audio: line (underruns must stay 0) and the audio sink: line (actual rate, divider, dma_underruns, mute state, source, volume).

    Not verified on hardware¶

    Everything in this page about the board's behaviour is from reading the datasheet-level behaviour of the RP2350 PIO and DMA and from the host build; none of it has been run on the Audio Pack. The 1 kHz tone on the Audio Pack is verified (3 Nov 2026). Specifically open: the DMA interrupt timing under UI load, clock jitter, 88.2/96 kHz, SD streaming throughput at 24/32-bit, the decoders' CPU load and flash-cache behaviour on the RP2350, the 16 KB core-1 stack margin under MP3 (watch core1_stack_free in the audio: line), and the real RAM headroom (about 28 KB were left in the link).
