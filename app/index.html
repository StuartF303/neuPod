    neuPod phone app: specification¶

    A music player for iOS and Android that looks and behaves like a neuPod, and later talks to one.

    Status: specification, 21 November 2026. Nothing is built. The working instructions for building it are in TEAM-BRIEF.md and the checklist is PROGRESS.md.

    Owner decisions (21 November 2026)¶

    1. The app is both a full player on the phone and, later, a companion to a neuPod device.
    2. Music comes from three places, in this order of delivery: files on the phone first; then the owner's NAS; then the card in a neuPod.
    3. Built in Flutter, one codebase for both platforms.
    4. iOS is built and tested for real: the owner has a Mac and an Apple developer account.

    Owner decisions in phase 0 (21 November 2026), after the platform research in decisions/:

    1. Ogg Vorbis and Opus on iOS are played through bundled Xiph decoders (libogg, libvorbis, libopus, BSD-3), because iOS has no decoder for them that apps can use.
    2. No Cast in R1. Casting from Android needs the app's own network connection. The output icon covers the phone speaker, wired, Bluetooth and, on iOS, AirPlay (the system does AirPlay's networking).
    3. iCloud files that are not downloaded are listed with a Download action. The app never fetches them itself; a tap asks iOS to download the file.

    4. Platform limits, accepted: on iOS a scan cannot keep running after the app is closed, so it resumes at the next launch (Android keeps going). Tracks from Android's shared music collection get embedded art only, because folder art would need a restricted image permission. Android 11+ cannot pick the storage root or Download, so the folder screen says so. Sample-rate passthrough is best effort and reported, never claimed (Android 14+ USB DACs where the phone supports it, an iOS preferred-rate hint, never over Bluetooth). The active output on Android 10 to 12 is inferred, because there is no public API before Android 13. MP3 and AAC files without encoder-delay metadata cannot be gapless on any engine.

    5. The app is the device (owner, 21 November 2026). The full-screen view is the front of a neuPod: the 4:3 screen above a touch click wheel with the four click buttons (Menu, previous, next, play/pause) and the centre button, in the device's proportions, scaled to the phone's width. Turning the wheel scrolls with haptic detents, and pressing a button or the centre gives a haptic click. It is drawn fresh, never copied from Apple's artwork, with no Apple logo and no "iPod"; the store-review risk stays with the owner at submission (Open question 1). This settles Open question 2 and most of Open question 3.

    6. The wheel sets the volume on Now Playing (owner, 21 November 2026), as on the device, on both platforms. It sets the phone's own system volume (no separate app volume): Android through AudioManager, iOS through a hidden MPVolumeView, the common workaround, since iOS has no public API for it. The device's volume bar replaces the progress bar while the wheel turns. If Apple breaks the workaround, the fallback is to be asked for, not guessed.
    7. Sort rule: the app matches the firmware's code (owner, 21 November 2026), lib_collate_key() in app/library/lib_collate.c, and the rule is written down under The library. The owner may change it later, in the firmware and the app together.
    8. Search is typed with the wheel (owner, 22 November 2026), as text is on the device. It never opens the phone's keyboard. It uses the firmware's text entry in search mode (docs/ui-input.md, model in app/include/wheel_input.h): the letter ring and mode chips, with live results as the user types. The chips are the firmware's six: abc, ABC, 123, #!?, space, del (owner, 22 November 2026; the design drew five, without #!?). The screen is in the device view design, "Search, by the wheel".

    9. Gate 2, first choices (owner, 22 November 2026), from the screenshots of the device view: fit A (the body fills the phone; device view design Open question 1), and the body finish follows the mode (silver in Light, black in Dark) with no separate Silver/Black setting (Open question 5). Also: the phone's own status bar is shown above the device; the neuPod wordmark stays on the body (Open question 8); rating stars live on Now Playing's page 2 only, as a Rating row set with the wheel; Search keeps its 24-character cap.

      Gate 2, remaining choices (owner, 22 November 2026). Device view Open questions: the accessibility text sizes stay capped at 5 rows (3); holding Play/Pause does nothing extra (4); our own proportions stay (6); routes: the phone speaker shows no icon, USB and other wired outputs show the jack plug, and AirPlay and other wireless outputs show a new generic external-speaker icon in the device's style (never Apple's AirPlay glyph) (7); a left-edge swipe is Menu on iOS (9); the click sound is a system sound that follows the ringer switch (10); Search stays in the Music menu (11); Search shows up to three live results, fewer at the larger text tiers (13). UI styles: Classic Light is adjusted to 4.5:1 where the designer's exact values fall short (the selection bar's upper stops under white text, rail letters, ink3 on grey surfaces), and Bluetooth saved versus active gets a cue besides colour; Home's right pane with nothing playing shows a placeholder cover, "Nothing playing" and the song count; tapping a side cover in Cover Flow centres it; the Music menu's preview pane shows for every item, not only Albums. Settings: the list under Settings plus the click sound switch (off by default) and a haptics switch. Direct touch (device view Open question 2) and drag-to-seek were judged by feel on the owner's phones at gate 1 and are settled by decision 18: candidate 2 (wheel plus screen), and the progress bar is not draggable. 14. Cover Flow matches the device (owner, 22 November 2026). Cover Flow is a second view of the Albums list, as on the device (app/ui/ui_library.c, LB-6 in the UI review): the same albums in the same order (by title, the firmware's Albums list order), not by artist then year. Under the centred album's title it shows the device's caption in both styles, Artist · Year · n songs as flow_texts builds it: no artist for a compilation, no year when there is none, 1 song or n songs, and no count when there are none. This replaces "albums by artist then year" here and the UI styles mock-ups' song count in Modern only. 15. Untagged files match the device (owner, 23 November 2026). A file's missing tags fall back as the firmware's library builder does (index_file in app/library/lib_build.c, ADR 0017 D5): the album is the name of the folder holding the file, the artist is Unknown Artist, and the title is the whole file name, extension included (01 Dust.flac). Nothing else is read from the path: there is no Artist/Year Album/NN Title.ext parsing (no artist, year or track number from folder or file names) and no , The handling of folder names. Album names or folders that differ only by (Disc n) stay separate albums. This replaces the folder-convention fallback proposed here. 16. Custom mode (owner, 25 November 2026). The mode setting becomes Light, Dark, Auto, Custom. Light, Dark and Auto are unchanged. Custom sets the colour and the transparency of the body, the wheel and the centre button separately, with a colour wheel as the picker; a custom look carries its own screen choice (Light, Dark or Auto); where a part is see-through a picture of a neuPod board shows from underneath, chosen from a small built-in set (only neuPod's own boards, never Apple's). Style stays a separate setting and works with all four modes. Three starter looks ship: Harbour, Sea glass and Clear case. The design is Custom mode (backlog AP-1); its other recommendations are proposals until the owner confirms them. 17. Nothing scrolls (owner, 25 November 2026). The app always fits the phone's screen and never shows a scroll bar, vertical or horizontal. Inside the device's 4:3 screen, new screens avoid scrolling too where they can. 18. Gate 1: touch and seek (owner, 22 to 23 November 2026), after the slice ran on an iPhone 15 Pro and a Pixel 7. Direct touch is candidate 2: the wheel plus taps and drags on the screen. Candidate 1 (wheel only) is removed. Seeking is by the wheel's seek mode only: the progress bar is not draggable. The iPhone survives a restart, the wheel feel and haptics are right, and the wheel sets the volume on Now Playing. 19. Unplayable songs (owner, 25 November 2026), from PR #159. A song is unplayable when the device would mark it damaged or the phone's player cannot decode it. Its row follows the UI styles design: greyed, with the reason at the right (Damaged file, Opus, can't play), and no toasts (the device today shows the codec and toasts the reason; the firmware follows the same design later). The selection bar lands on it, as on the device, and Select opens nothing. Unplayable songs are left out of the queue, so none stops it, and Shuffle Songs never starts on one (backlog UI-24 for the device). 20. Ratings and play counts (owner, 25 November 2026), from PR #160 (decision 0011). - Identity: a track's ratings and play counts are keyed as on the device, by the file's path and size, and a rescan that sees a file move (the old path gone and one new file with the same audio fingerprint) carries them to the new key. The fingerprint has no tag bytes in it, so a retag that changes the size carries them too (FLAC, MP3, MP4/M4A, WAV; Ogg, Opus and raw AAC files are not opened by the scanner, as on the device, and get no carry-over). - Skip counts are dropped for now, on the app and the device (the firmware has none). - Backups: ratings and play counts are kept in their own small database, which iCloud and Google backups include; the library database, which a rescan rebuilds, stays out of backups. 21. Android's shared music collection (owner, 26 November 2026), from PR #162 (decision 0012). - Duplicates: a file inside a picked folder is listed once, by the folder; the collection leaves it out. Adding or removing a folder over songs already listed carries their ratings and play counts to the new path (decision 20's carry-over). - Music only: the collection lists what Android marks as music (IS_MUSIC); ringtones, notification and alarm sounds, recordings and podcasts are left out. - Words: Settings > Library shows Add Music on This Phone while it is off; once on, it is listed as a folder is, as Music on This Phone, with Turn Off where a folder has Remove (asking first, as Remove does).

    Everything else in this document is the designer's proposal until the owner confirms it. Proposals that most need a decision are collected under Open questions.

    What it is for¶

    The owner is re-ripping about 300 CDs to FLAC as the library for the neuPod. The app gives the same library, the same menus and the same look on the phone he already carries, and becomes the easy way to manage a neuPod once the device has a network interface.

    Goals

    • Play a large lossless library from the phone, gaplessly, in the background, with the controls people expect on the lock screen and on headsets.
    • Feel like a neuPod: the same menu structure, vocabulary and visual styles (Classic and Modern), adapted to touch and to a portrait screen.
    • Work fully offline with no account.

    Not goals

    • Streaming services, a store, social features, recommendations, lyrics, podcasts (podcasts are a later device feature).
    • Video.
    • Editing tags or files. The app reads the library; it never changes the owner's music files.
    • Tablets and desktop layouts in the first release.

    It has failed if

    • it ever alters, moves or deletes a music file without being asked;
    • playback gaps between tracks of a continuous album, or stops when the screen locks;
    • it sends anything about the library off the phone;
    • it looks like a generic player with a neuPod logo on it.

    Release What it adds Depends on

    R2: NAS Browse, stream and download from a home server An owner decision on the server (see Open questions); R1

    R2 and R3 are outlined at the end so R1 is built with room for them. They are not to be started without the owner.

    R1: the local player¶

    Getting music onto the phone¶

    • Android: the user picks one or more folders with the system folder picker; the app keeps read access to them. It also offers the device's shared music collection.
    • iOS: the app has its own Documents folder, visible in the Files app and in Finder when the phone is plugged into a Mac, so a library is copied in by dragging folders. The user can also pick a folder elsewhere in Files (for example on iCloud Drive or an attached drive) and the app keeps access to it.
    • The app never copies music it can read in place, and never writes into a music folder.

    Formats¶

    Must play: FLAC (up to 24-bit / 192 kHz), ALAC, MP3, AAC (M4A), WAV, Opus and Ogg Vorbis. These match the device's required codecs. A file the platform cannot decode is listed greyed with the reason at the right (Opus, can't play), exactly as the device does; it is never hidden and never stops the queue.

    The library¶

    • Built by scanning the chosen folders and reading tags: title, artist, album artist, album, track and disc number, year, genre, compilation flag, duration, sample rate and bit depth, embedded art.
    • Where tags are missing, the device's fallback (owner decision 15): the album is the folder's name, the artist Unknown Artist, the title the whole file name (extension included); nothing else is taken from the path. Folder.jpg (or cover.jpg) is the album art.
    • The discs of one album (one album tag, disc number tags) are one album with disc sections, as on the device; album names or folders that differ only by (Disc n) stay apart, as there.
    • A track whose album artist is Various Artists, or whose compilation flag is set, belongs to Compilations and is not listed under each contributing artist's albums.
    • The scan runs in the background, shows progress, can be cancelled, and survives the app being closed (on iOS it resumes at the next launch; owner decision 8). A rescan only reads files that are new or changed. Removing a folder removes its tracks from the library and nothing else.
    • Sizing target: 20,000 tracks. First scan of 5,000 FLAC files in under five minutes on a mid-range phone; a rescan with no changes in under ten seconds. (Targets are proposals; measure and report.)
    • Sort rule (owner decision 11): names sort by a collation key built as the firmware's lib_collate_key() does. A sort tag is used when present, otherwise the name. One leading article is skipped, in any case, when something follows it: The, A or An ("The Beatles" under B, "A Perfect Circle" under P). Letters compare without case and with accents folded. Order: symbols first, then digits, then letters. The fast-scroll letter is the first letter of the key, # for a digit or symbol. docs/ui.md still says "A "/"An " are not skipped; the code is the reference until the owner changes both.
    • Ratings (0 to 5), play counts and last played are kept per track in the app's own database, keyed as on the device (path and size), and survive a rescan, a file move within the library and a retag; that database is in phone backups (owner decision 20). Skip counts were dropped (owner decision 20: the device has none).

    Browsing¶

    The same structure as the device:

    • Home: Music, Playlists, Settings, Shuffle Songs, Now Playing (only while something is loaded).
    • Music: Cover Flow, Playlists, Artists, Albums, Songs, Genres, Compilations.
    • An artist: All Songs, then the albums with cover, year and song count.
    • An album: header with the cover, artist, year, song count and total time; numbered tracks, with disc headings.
    • Songs: two lines per row, title over artist and album.
    • Cover Flow: a second view of the Albums list, in its order (by title), with the device's caption Artist · Year · n songs under the centred album (owner decision 14); the centred album opens on tap.
    • Fast scroll: a letter index on long lists.
    • Search, typed with the wheel (owner decision 12): the field, the letter ring turned by the wheel with the mode chips under it, and the best matches live as the user types; Play/Pause goes to the full results, grouped as artists, albums and songs, and Menu returns to typing with the text kept. It never opens the phone's keyboard.

    Long names end in an ellipsis in lists and scroll as a ticker where the device scrolls them (the focused title on Now Playing).

    Playback¶

    • A play queue with Play, Play Next and Add to Queue from any track, album, artist, genre or playlist. The queue is visible and can be reordered and cleared.
    • Gapless between consecutive tracks of the same format; verified by test, not by ear alone.
    • Shuffle (off, on) and repeat (off, all, one), with the same glyphs as the device's status bar.
    • Seek with the wheel's seek mode; the progress bar is not draggable (owner decision 18). Skip back restarts the track after three seconds, otherwise goes to the previous one.
    • Background playback with lock-screen and notification controls, artwork and scrubbing; headset and Bluetooth transport buttons; correct behaviour on interruptions (a call pauses; unplugging headphones pauses; another app taking audio pauses).
    • Resume: reopening the app restores the queue, the track and the position.
    • Volume is the phone's own volume. The app has no volume of its own; on Now Playing the wheel sets the system volume and shows the device's volume bar (owner decision 10).
    • The output in use (phone speaker, wired, Bluetooth or AirPlay) is shown by an icon, as on the device. No Cast in R1 (owner decision 6).
    • No resampling or processing in the app. Where the platform lets the app pass the file's sample rate through to an external DAC, do so and say what was achieved in the format line; do not claim bit-perfect output that has not been measured.

    Playlists¶

    • On-The-Go: add from any row; reorder; clear; save as a named playlist.
    • Named playlists: create, rename, reorder, delete.
    • Import and export .m3u8, with paths relative to the library folder, so playlists can move between the phone and a neuPod's card.

    Now Playing¶

    Art, title, artist, album and year, the small format line (FLAC 16/44.1), progress with elapsed and remaining, shuffle and repeat, rating stars, and a route to the queue. In Modern the backdrop and the accent come from the album art, by the same rule as the device: the dominant colour, clamped until white text on it reaches 4.5:1.

    Look and feel¶

    • Two styles, Classic and Modern, each in Light and Dark, with the colours and skins in the UI styles design. Mode follows the phone's setting by default; style is chosen in Settings.
    • Custom mode (owner decision 16). A fourth mode in which the user sets the colour and transparency of the body, the wheel and the centre button, with a board picture showing through a see-through part. The editor, the rule that keeps the legends at 4.5:1 on any colour, the board pictures and the data model are in the Custom mode design.
    • The device view (owner decision 9). The phone shows the device's front: the 4:3 screen, running the device's screens with the UI styles design's values (drawn at 600x450, so they carry over), above the click wheel. The wheel behaves as on the device: turning scrolls, with acceleration and the fast-scroll letter tile on long lists; the centre is Select; Menu goes back; previous, next and play/pause work as on the device, including press-and-hold to scan. Each wheel detent is a light haptic tick, and each button press is a firmer haptic click. An optional click sound, like the original's clicker, can be turned on in Settings.
    • The layout and the wheel's numbers are in the device view design: geometry as ratios of the phone's width, the fit on tall and short phones, body finishes, hit zones, press-or-turn, detents and acceleration (a port of the firmware's ui_accel_feed()), holds, haptics, direct touch, the three text tiers, accessibility and the phone's status bar. Its recommendations (the fit, the body finish, direct touch, the phone's status bar shown, the large-text tiers, the wordmark) were proposals for the owner, settled at gate 2 (owner decision 13); direct touch and drag-to-seek were settled at gate 1 (owner decision 18).
    • Search is typed with the wheel's letter ring, never the phone's keyboard (owner decision 12). It sits in the Music menu (device view design Open question 11, owner decision 13).
    • Type is Montserrat (SIL OFL), icons are Lucide (ISC), as on the device. Every third-party asset and package is listed on an in-app Licences screen.
    • Feedback follows the device's three places where they translate: a row confirms with a brief flash and a tick; a greyed row carries its reason; something lost or about to be lost gets a centred panel. No toasts for routine things.

    Settings¶

    Style; mode (Light, Dark, Auto, Custom; owner decision 16); Custom look, the list of up to six custom looks and their editor (Custom mode design); library folders (add, remove, rescan); gapless on or off; skip-back behaviour; click sound (off by default, as the device view design says); haptics (the design's rule: off whenever the phone's own setting is off); reset ratings and play counts (with a panel to confirm); About; Licences (owner decision 13).

    Accessibility¶

    The wheel and buttons have screen-reader equivalents (the list is navigable row by row, and the wheel is an adjustable control with actions for Select, Menu, previous, next and play/pause). Every control has a screen-reader label; the app honours the system text size up to the largest standard setting without clipping; text contrast is at least 4.5:1 in every style and mode; nothing depends on colour alone; touch targets are at least 44 points.

    Privacy and safety¶

    • R1 makes no network connection at all. No analytics, no crash reporting service, no accounts. iOS downloading an iCloud file after the user taps Download (owner decision 7), and AirPlay (decision 6), are the system's networking, not the app's.
    • The app requests only the permissions it needs to read the chosen folders, play in the background, show media controls and, on Android, show the background library scan's notification (decision 0009).
    • Signing keys, provisioning profiles and store credentials are never committed. The repository is public.

    How it is built¶

    • Flutter, current stable, one codebase in mobile/ at the repository root. Licence MPL-2.0, like the firmware.
    • Layers, each testable without the ones above it: a pure-Dart domain (library model, queue, shuffle and repeat rules, sorting and naming rules); data (database, scanner, tag reader, art cache); audio (the player and the platform media session); UI.
    • Packages (audio engine, media session, tag reading, database, state management) are the team's choice. Each choice is recorded in a short decision note with the alternatives considered. A package must be actively maintained, licensed compatibly with MPL-2.0 distribution on both stores, and checked against its current documentation, not chosen from memory.
    • The domain rules that the device also implements (sort order, the untagged fallback, compilation rules, queue behaviour) follow the firmware's behaviour as documented in UI and the ADRs, so a library looks the same in both.

    How it is tested¶

    Level What Where
    Unit Domain rules, scanner, tag fallbacks, playlist import and export, the accent-colour rule Every PR
    Widget and golden Every screen in Classic and Modern, Light and Dark, at a small and a large phone size and at the largest text size Every PR
    Integration Pick a folder, scan, browse, play, background, resume; on an Android emulator and an iOS simulator Every PR (CI: Linux for Android, a Mac runner for iOS)
    Audio Decode each fixture in tests/assets/audio and compare with its checksum where the platform allows; gapless join measured on a generated two-track fixture CI where possible, otherwise the bench
    Device The owner's iPhone and an Android phone: real library, lock screen, headset buttons, Bluetooth, interruptions, an hour of continuous play End of each phase, recorded with the date
    Scale A generated 20,000-track library: scan time, memory, scroll smoothness End of the library phase

    The repository's audio fixtures (tests/assets/audio) are reused; a generated library with the owner's folder convention and awkward names is added for scanner tests. The owner's own music is never committed.

    R2 outline: NAS¶

    Browse and play from a home server; download albums for offline use; show clearly what is on the phone and what is remote. R1 keeps the library model source-neutral (a track has a source and a location) so this is an addition, not a rewrite. The server type is an open question.

    R3 outline: companion¶

    Discover a neuPod on the local network; show its battery, storage and what it is playing; remote control; browse and play its card; send albums and playlists to it and remove them; exchange ratings and play counts; set its settings; later, firmware update. All of this needs the device's HTTP API. That API is to be specified in an ADR written with the firmware, not invented by the app. Wi-Fi currently describes that step as "device HTTP API and a C# companion app for library sync"; whether a separate desktop companion is still wanted is an open question.

    Open questions¶

    Not decided. Do not settle these by guessing.
